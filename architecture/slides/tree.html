Why no vector database?

Laws already have a table of contents. We use it.

The usual way

  • Cut documents into chunks
  • Turn each chunk into numbers (embeddings)
  • Run a vector database to search them
  • Chunks can cut across articles

The LexTreeRAG way

  • Each law is split by its own articles
  • AI reads the list of articles: about 3,000 tokens for a 60-article law
  • Opens only articles that score 4 or more out of 10
  • Answers cite exact articles. Nothing extra to run.
Most AI search tools cut documents into small chunks, turn them into numbers and need a special database to search them. Laws do not need that. They already have articles and a clear structure. So the AI reads the list of articles with a one-line summary each, which is small and cheap, and picks the ones that matter. The result: answers that point to exact articles, and one less system to run and pay for.
